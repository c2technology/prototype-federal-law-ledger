# June 1, 1789 Oaths Act source review

This review is the verification checklist for issue #1. It distinguishes the 1789 effective-law baseline from later codification descendants.

## Expected enacted document

- [x] Official title: **An Act to regulate the Time and Manner of administering certain Oaths.**[1][2]
- [x] Congress and session: First Congress, first session.[1][2]
- [x] Chapter: 1.[1][2]
- [x] Approval date: June 1, 1789.[1][6]
- [x] Statutes at Large citation: 1 Stat. 23–24.[1]
- [x] Five numbered sections.[1]
- [x] Text compared paragraph by paragraph against the chapter PDF and the image of printed page 23, rather than accepted from OCR without review.[1][21][22]
- [x] The continuation on printed page 24 was checked against its page image.[23]
- [x] Editorial square brackets in the Statutes at Large printing retained rather than silently removed.[1]

## Legislative record checks

- [x] The House record names the five Representatives ordered to prepare and bring in the bill, but the checked sources do not designate one modern-style primary sponsor.[3]
- [x] Those committee members are resolved by full name against the First Federal Congress member list and described only by their documented role; they are not relabeled as sponsors or cosponsors.[24]
- [x] The Senate considered committee amendments on May 4, 1789.[4]
- [x] The Senate passed the bill with amendments on May 5, 1789; the journal entry supplies no member-level roll call.[5]
- [x] No individual House or Senate vote positions are inferred.
- [x] President George Washington's signature on June 1, 1789 is supported by the House historian and the enrolled Act.[6][7]

## Disposition checks

- [x] The effective tree represents law as it stood immediately after June 1, 1789, not modern Code text imported out of chronological order.
- [x] The architecture explains why an Act enacted before the United States Code begins in `effective/uncodified/`.
- [x] OLRC Table III's later classification chain is documented: section 2 to Revised Statutes sections 28–30 and section 3 to Revised Statutes sections 1836–1837.[8]
- [x] Current descendants of Revised Statutes sections 28–30 are documented as 2 U.S.C. §§ 21, 22, and 25.[9][10][11]
- [x] Current 4 U.S.C. §§ 101–102 are documented as later positive-law restatements whose derivation chain runs through Revised Statutes sections 1836–1837; they are not inserted into the 1789 effective tree.[12][13][14]
- [x] Sections 1, 4, and 5 are not assigned a Code destination merely because Table III has no entry for them.
- [x] Any unresolved later repeal, supersession, or omission is stated as future chronological work rather than guessed.

## Historical gaps to preserve

- No modern bill number was found in the checked sources.
- No source checked designates one individual as primary sponsor.
- No source checked identifies official cosponsors.
- No complete introduced text or ordered series of official bill versions was found.
- No member-level House or Senate passage tally was found.
- The checked records support a House preparation committee and Senate amendments, but they do not support treating committee membership as sponsorship or reconstructing each amendment as a separate text revision.

## Current disposition research

The effective file in this PR is intentionally a June 1, 1789 historical state. It does not assert that the original Act remains effective today.

| 1789 section | Later official disposition |
|---|---|
| 1 | No direct descendant appears in OLRC Table III. Its immediate-administration language was temporary. The current federal oath in 5 U.S.C. §3331 derives from later legislation rather than citing this section.[8][18][19][20] |
| 2 | OLRC maps it to Revised Statutes §§28–30. Those sections are now classified to 2 U.S.C. §§21, 22, and 25, whose codification notes cite the 1789 section.[8][9][10][11] |
| 3 | OLRC maps it to Revised Statutes §§1836–1837.[8][15] The 1947 positive-law enactment of Title 4 carried those rules through former Title 4 §§9–10 into current 4 U.S.C. §§101–102.[12][13][14][17] |
| 4 | No direct descendant appears in Table III. Later federal oath legislation addressed the subject, and current 5 U.S.C. §3331 does not cite the 1789 section in its source credit.[8][18][19][20] |
| 5 | No direct descendant appears in Table III. Current 2 U.S.C. §25 derives its Clerk language from section 2 through Revised Statutes §30, rather than from section 5's separate oath.[8][11] |

Revised Statutes §5596, enacted June 22, 1874, repealed pre-December 1, 1873 Acts having any portion embraced in the revision. Because OLRC identifies sections 2 and 3 as embraced in Revised Statutes sections, the original Act has no present uncodified residue.[8][16] That later repeal and the descendants above belong in their own chronological enactment PRs; importing them now would make the 1789 effective state false.
